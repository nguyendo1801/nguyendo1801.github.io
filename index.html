§ 01 · About

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer and Information Science and Engineering at the University of Florida. I work where learning meets structured decision-making: models that must perform well and also make reliable, explainable decisions under uncertainty. I care about AI that is not only intelligent but intelligible, scalable and theoretically sound, and that holds up beyond software, in networks and in hardware.

§ 03 · Highlighted projects

Highlighted projects.

Journal in preparation

Rare critical driving scenes, from simulator to the real world

Crashes and near misses are rare in real driving logs, which is exactly when autonomous vehicles need data most. We systematically generate rare, safety-critical scenes in simulators (CARLA, BeamNG) and transfer them to photorealistic real-world video, keeping the scene faithful across all six cameras (multi-view consistency), over time (temporal consistency), and around the critical event itself (event preservation), so the obstacle that causes the crash stays where the simulator put it.

Semantic Optimal Transport

Matching SAE features across layers and compressing circuits.

Each sparse-autoencoder feature becomes an activation-weighted cloud of the hidden states that fire it. Clouds from two layers are placed in one reference space and compared by optimal transport; the same distance groups a circuit into supernodes.

58.6 vs 20.4 LLM-judged match accuracy for FeatFlow (Gemma-2-2B, layer 12→13)

Paper ↗
AISTATS 2026

Q-ShiftDP

Differential privacy built for quantum gradients.

Parameter-shift gradients come from two shifted circuit runs and are bounded by the observable’s spectrum, so no clipping is needed. Finite-shot noise already randomizes them, so less Gaussian noise is added for the same (ε, δ).

Δ bound ‖g‖ ≤ (λmax − λmin)/2 · √ΣΩ²: the sensitivity comes for free

Paper ↗
AAAI 2026 · co-first author

Hexaïssa

Standing on giants’ shoulders: routing the best chess engines.

A mixture-of-experts router reads the position and hands the move to one of several strong chess engines, guided by a reward learned in latent space. (Illustration based on the paper’s title.)

MoE router over expert engines with latent reward learning

NeurIPS 2025 · first author

Hephaestus

How cheaply can slowed links break quality of service?

Forge: a path-stressing algorithm with a guarantee raises edge weights until every critical pair’s shortest path passes the threshold. Morph: a mixture of conditional VAEs learns those solutions, adding experts where it misses the energy model. Refine: an RL agent moves in latent space toward cheaper feasible attacks.

2.20M vs 3.01M total attack cost for DIFFILO on Skitter (linear weights, T = 260%)

Paper ↗
ICLR 2025 · first author

Swift Hydra

Training detectors on anomalies they have never seen.

An RL policy shifts the latent code of a conditional VAE to create diverse anomalies that slip past the detector. The best ones are added to training, the boundary tightens, and a mixture of Mamba experts (top-2 per input) keeps inference fast.

0.93 vs 0.80 AUC-ROC for a vanilla Mamba detector on ADBench (40/60 split)

Paper ↗
AAAI 2025 · first author

REM

Reinforced multi-expert influence maximization.

A VAE (Seed2Vec) acts as the policy: its latent codes decode to seed sets. Gradient ascent on spread predicted by a propagation mixture of GNN experts finds better seeds; the best go to a priority memory and retrain both models.

804K users reached vs 787K for MIM-Reasoner (ParisAttack2015, LT, 20% budget)

Paper ↗
AISTATS 2024 · first author

MIM-Reasoner

Influence across many social networks, with a guarantee.

A knapsack splits the seed budget across layers; one RL agent per layer picks seeds, discounting users already reached through other layers, and a Chow-Liu graphical model captures how influence crosses layers.

53.4K users reached vs 49.3K for KSN on Twitter-Foursquare, with 1.81 s inference
